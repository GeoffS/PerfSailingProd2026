A blokart® Downhaul with a Downwind Preset Control

Over the past year I have been working to "keep my head out of the boat" as much as possible. One part of that process has been simplifying my use of the downhaul to the following steps:

  • Approaching the leeward mark: pull on downhaul fairly tight.
  • After rounding and heading upwind: adjust to conditions (if necessary).
  • Approaching the windward mark: release the downhaul fully.
  • After rounding and heading downwind: pull some downhaul if necessary.

With a little bit of practice, I can perform both the "approaching the mark" adjustments in just a second or two without having to look at the downhaul control line or the sail.

Looking at the videos from the 2014 blokart Worlds in Australia and other races, I noticed that some of the Spanish sailors had two downhaul control lines. They are using a cascade tackle and one control was a coarse adjustment from the upper 2:1 purchase and the other came from the lower 3:1 purchase and had the normal full 6:1 mechanical advantage.

For most of last summer I sailed with a variety of cascade downhauls. They were all very successful, but in the late fall I decided to experiment with more conventional triple block systems since they were more common and I wanted to get more experience with them.

Since sometimes you want to have some downhaul tension going downwind, one improvement I tried was to lead the other end of the purchse (the end that would normally be fixed to the becket on the lower block) to a cleat on the side-tube. I use this only to adjust the length of the downhaul when I unlceat the primary control and let it run out to the stopper ball. This lets me eliminate the "After rounding and heading downwind" adjustment step unless the wind strength has dramatically changed.
